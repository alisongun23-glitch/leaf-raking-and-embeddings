# Local / cluster-specific origins, frames and subspaces in embedding spaces

Scope: prior work that moves the origin to a *local* centre (k-NN centroid, cluster mean, anchor set) or builds a per-region frame/subspace for word, token or sentence embeddings. For each match the entry states: technique; what "centre" it uses; whether it reads *meaning* off local directions or only *fixes distances*; measured effect. Research date: 2026-11-01.

Short answer up front: "move the origin to each cluster's centre" exists in at least three separate literatures — **localized centering / DisSimLocal** (hubness reduction, k-NN centroid, distance-fixing only), **cluster-based isotropy correction** (k-means mean subtraction + local PCA, directions *removed* not interpreted), and **relative/anchor representations** (origin replaced by an anchor set, distance-fixing/invariance only). PCA on hand-picked sets with interpretation of the top component exists (gender subspace), and per-category projection onto hand-picked axes exists (semantic projection). I found **no** paper that centres a hand-picked semantic field at its own mean, runs PCA on that neighbourhood, and interprets the resulting eigenvectors as meanings.

## KQ1. Localized centering (Hara et al. 2015, 2016) and hubness in word embeddings

### Takeaway
Localized centering replaces the global centroid with each point's k-NN centroid and subtracts the point's similarity to that local centroid from every similarity score; it is a similarity *correction*, explicitly *not* an origin shift, and it reads no meaning off local directions. It lowers hubness skewness strongly on text (tf-idf document) data, but a later 50-dataset benchmark found it the weakest hubness reducer overall, with DisSimLocal (the Euclidean variant, same local centroid) among the best.

### Cited Findings
- **Technique (Hara et al., AAAI 2015).** Local centroid c_κ(x) = mean of x's κ nearest neighbours ("local segment size" κ, distinct from the classifier's k). Localized centering: Sim_LCENT(x;q) = ⟨x,q⟩ − ⟨x,c_κ(x)⟩, generalised to ⟨x,q⟩ − ⟨x,c_κ(x)⟩^γ with γ tuned to minimise skewness of the k-occurrence distribution N_k. When κ = |D| it reduces to ordinary centering. — [Hara et al. 2015 full text](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/AAAI/2015/localized_centering_reducing_hubness_in_largesample_data__1e4baab2.txt); [AAAI page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9629)
- **Why it reduces hubness.** Two regimes: in high-dimensional data hubs are points close to the *global* centroid ("spatial centrality"), which ordinary centering removes; in large-sample data (n ≈ 10,000, d ≈ 500) hubs are points similar to their *local* centroid, so global centering fails. "Local affinity" ⟨x,c_κ(x)⟩ correlates with N_k in both regimes, so subtracting it targets both. — [Hara et al. 2015](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/AAAI/2015/localized_centering_reducing_hubness_in_largesample_data__1e4baab2.txt)
- **Not an origin shift; distance-fixing only.** The authors state that unlike standard centering, the transformation "cannot be interpreted as shifting the origin of the vector space," the resulting similarity is asymmetric (Sim_LCENT(x;y) ≠ Sim_LCENT(y;x)), κ and γ must be tuned, and no theoretical analysis is given ("A theoretical analysis of hubness reduction with localized centering shall be pursued in future work"). — [Hara et al. 2015](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/AAAI/2015/localized_centering_reducing_hubness_in_largesample_data__1e4baab2.txt)
- **Measured effect (text data, tf-idf documents, k=10).** Skewness of N_k, cosine → centering → localized centering: WebKB 2.64 → 2.85 → 1.13; Reuters-52 14.82 → 11.04 → 1.76; TDT2-30 3.63 → 3.18 → 0.91; 20Newsgroups 3.25 → 2.99 → 0.81. kNN accuracy: Reuters-52 0.872 → 0.885 → 0.901; 20Newsgroups 0.859 → 0.860 → 0.877; negligible on TDT2-30 (0.964 → 0.963 → 0.964). Local scaling was competitive and sometimes slightly more accurate (WebKB 0.764 vs 0.761). — [Hara et al. 2015](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/AAAI/2015/localized_centering_reducing_hubness_in_largesample_data__1e4baab2.txt)
- **Precursor: global centering as origin shift (Suzuki, Hara, Shimbo, Saerens, Fukumizu, EMNLP 2013).** Subtracting the dataset centroid ("shifting the origin of the vector space") reduces hubs under inner-product similarity; identified by Hara 2015 as the base case of localized centering. — [ACL Anthology D13-1058](https://aclanthology.org/D13-1058/); characterised in [Hara et al. 2015](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/AAAI/2015/localized_centering_reducing_hubness_in_largesample_data__1e4baab2.txt)
- **Hara et al. AAAI 2016 "Flattening the density gradient".** Spatial centrality (points near the data centre are close to everything) is treated as a source of hubness; centering does nothing under Euclidean distance because pairwise distances are unchanged, so they subtract a per-sample centrality term from squared Euclidean distance, which "flattens the density gradient"; improves kNN classification and beats an existing hub-reduction method. — [AAAI page](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/10240) (abstract via search; full text blocked by bot protection)
- **DisSimLocal (the Euclidean version).** Defined in the Feldbauer & Flexer benchmark as squared Euclidean distance between x and y minus the squared distances from x and from y to their own local centroids c_k(·) = mean of k nearest neighbours. — [Feldbauer & Flexer, KAIS 2019 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7327987/); implementation with k = number of neighbours for local centroids (default 5), citing Hara et al. 2016 — [scikit-hubness docs](https://scikit-hubness.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/_autosummary/skhubness.reduction.DisSimLocal.html)
- **Independent benchmark (50 datasets, 12 methods).** DisSimLocal gave the strongest hubness reduction (MP, simhubIN, LS, NICDM, SNN statistically tied with it); *localized centering increased hubness on average* and was "the weakest method for hubness reduction," though competitive on kNN accuracy "mainly on text data." Centering "works mainly on text data." Recommendation: MP, LS, NICDM or DSL for general use. No word-embedding datasets were included (text datasets were bag-of-words: mini-newsgroups, DMOZ, Amazon reviews, rcv1, sector…). — [Feldbauer & Flexer 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7327987/)
- **Hubness in word-embedding mapping (Dinu, Lazaridou & Baroni 2015).** In zero-shot cross-lingual/cross-modal mapping, nearest-neighbour lists are "strongly polluted by hubs"; their fix re-ranks candidates using the proximity distribution of each candidate across many mapped vectors (a global, not local-centroid, correction) with "consistent improvements." — [arXiv 1412.6568](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.6568)
- **Hubs in token-embedding ID estimation (2026 preprint).** A hub of short-norm token rows inflates intrinsic-dimension estimates of embedding tables; the paper attributes the first observation of hubness in word-embedding spaces to Dinu et al. 2015. — [arXiv 2608.29702](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29702) (search snippet; not fully read)

### Inferences
- In the hubness literature the local centroid is purely a nuisance term: it is subtracted from a *scalar* similarity, never used to define a local basis. Nothing in this line interprets directions relative to c_κ(x).
- The 2015 paper's own remark that localized centering "cannot be interpreted as shifting the origin" is the clearest statement that the learner's idea (a genuine per-cluster coordinate system) is *not* what localized centering does; DisSimLocal is closer to a per-point origin shift in squared-distance space but is still only a distance correction.
- Evidence that localized centering helps *word* embeddings specifically is absent from the sources I could read: Hara 2015 used tf-idf documents, Feldbauer & Flexer used no word-embedding sets.

### Gaps
- Hara et al. 2016 full text was not readable (AAAI site blocked automated access); the density-gradient derivation and exact numbers are unverified beyond the abstract.
- I found no study applying localized centering or DisSimLocal to word2vec/GloVe/BERT neighbour search. CSLS (Conneau et al. 2018) is the standard local hubness correction for word-embedding retrieval, and a search result described it as relying on "a local measure of hubness," but I did not fetch the primary paper, so its exact relation to localized centering (it uses mean cosine to k nearest neighbours of both query and target) is not verified here — [search-result mention](https://awesomepapers.io/speech-audio/papers/2605.26575).
- Radovanović et al. 2010 "Hubs in space" was not fetched; its role (hubness as a high-dimensional phenomenon tied to proximity to the data mean) is only reflected through Hara 2015's framing.

## KQ2. Cluster-based centering / local isotropy (Rajaee & Pilehvar 2021; Cai et al. 2021; related centering work)

### Takeaway
Rajaee & Pilehvar's method is the most literal prior instance of "move the origin to each cluster's centre": k-means → subtract each cluster's mean → local PCA → remove the top principal components per cluster. It raises isotropy and STS scores beyond global all-but-the-top, and they *did* inspect what the local dominant directions encode (syntax/structure, verb tense) — but only in order to delete them, not to use them as semantic axes. Later work (IsoScore, Mickus et al. 2024) warns that isotropy is mean-agnostic and that global isotropy is mathematically at odds with cluster structure, which cuts against treating cluster-wise whitening as unambiguously good.

### Cited Findings
- **Rajaee & Pilehvar (ACL 2021) technique.** Plain k-means with k tuned in [5,30] on STS-B dev (10 for GPT-2, 27 for BERT and RoBERTa); each cluster made zero-mean by subtracting *that cluster's mean* (following Mu & Viswanath 2018); PCA on each zero-mean cluster and removal of its top components (12 for BERT/RoBERTa, 30 for GPT-2). — [ar5iv 2106.01183](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.01183); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-short.73)
- **Centre = k-means cluster mean; directions = cluster-local PCs (removed).** Clustering plus per-cluster zero-meaning alone (k=20) raises the partition-function isotropy I(W) for BERT from 5.05E-05 to 0.262 and RoBERTa from 2.70E-06 to 0.603; GPT-2 only from 5.02E-174 to 4.06E-47 ("far from isotropic"). — [ar5iv 2106.01183](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.01183)
- **Measured effect vs global method.** I(W) on STS-B, baseline → global (Mu & Viswanath) → cluster-based: GPT-2 2.6E-105 → 0.51 → 0.70; BERT 1.1E-04 → 0.58 → 0.73; RoBERTa 2.9E-06 → 0.88 → 0.90. STS-B Spearman: GPT-2 24.8 → 55.7 → 62.3; BERT 47.4 → 65.5 → 66.0; RoBERTa 56.0 → 70.1 → 71.4. BERT classification average (RTE, CoLA, SST-2, MRPC, WiC, BoolQ): 61.2 → 62.1 → 63.2. — [ar5iv 2106.01183](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.01183)
- **What the local dominant directions encode (inspected, then discarded).** Dominant local directions in punctuation and stop-word clusters carry "structural and syntactic information about the sentences in which they appear" (removing them reduced same-structure nearest neighbours in a Ravfogel et al. 2020 probe); in verb clusters tense dominates sense, and after removal same-sense verbs in different tenses move closer. — [ar5iv 2106.01183](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.01183)
- **Limitations stated/implicit.** Hyperparameters tuned on STS-B dev; classification only on BERT; GPT-2 needs 30 removed directions; cosine deemed unreliable as an isotropy measure so I(W) used; fine-tuning effects left to future work. — [ar5iv 2106.01183](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.01183)
- **Cai, Huang, Bian & Church (ICLR 2021).** Contextual embeddings (BERT, ERNIE) are globally anisotropic (vectors in a narrow cone, high cosines), but "isotropy does exist" from a different perspective: the authors "identify isolated clusters and low dimensional manifolds in the contextual embedding space, and introduce tools to both qualitatively and quantitatively analyze them." — [ICLR 2021 poster page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2021/poster/3079); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=xYGNO86OWDH) (full text blocked)
- **Rajaee & Pilehvar's reading of Cai et al.** The space "is extremely anisotropic in all non-input layers from a global sight, but is significantly more isotropic locally when embeddings are clustered and each cluster is made zero-mean." — [search summary citing ACL 2021 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-short.73)
- **Liang et al. 2021 "Learning to Remove".** Removes several dominant directions of BERT embeddings with learnable weights trained on word-similarity tasks (global, not cluster-wise); BERT word vectors "are not centered at the origin" and sit in a narrow cone; average +16% on STS over the original and two baselines. — [arXiv 2104.05274](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05274)
- **Timkey & van Schijndel 2021 "rogue dimensions".** 1–3 (up to 5) dimensions dominate cosine in BERT/RoBERTa/GPT-2/XLNet (one XLNet dimension >99% of expected cosine between random tokens); static word2vec/GloVe are comparatively isotropic. Fix: per-dimension standardisation (centre on mean, divide by std) — a *global* origin shift plus rescaling — improves Spearman with human similarity (RG65, WS353, SimLex999, SimVerb3500) in nearly all layers; the rogue dimensions tie to punctuation/position, not broad linguistic knowledge. — [ar5iv 2109.04404](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.04404)
- **Which global mean? (Oyama, Yokoi & Shimodaira 2023).** For SGNS, 2·KL(p(·|w)‖p(·)) ≃ û_wᵀ G û_w where û_w = u_w − ū and ū = Σ_w p(w)u_w is the *frequency-weighted* mean embedding (stands in for the embedding of the unigram distribution); centering changes the fit slope from 0.048 (raw norm) to 0.909 (u₀ estimated from "the/of/and") or 1.384 (ū). Centred squared norm gives best hypernym discrimination (68.84 avg accuracy vs 59.48 raw norm, 50 chance). — [arXiv 2212.09663 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.09663)
- **Zipfian whitening (Yokoi et al. 2024).** Most symmetry-correction methods "implicitly assume that the word frequencies are uniform"; PCA whitening weighted by empirical Zipfian word frequency "significantly improves task performance, surpassing established baselines." — [arXiv 2411.00680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00680)
- **Isotropy is mean-agnostic (IsoScore).** Rudman et al. argue the partition score returns ≈1 whenever data are zero-mean, and that "isotropy is strictly a property of the covariance matrix of a distribution and is by definition mean-agnostic." — [arXiv 2108.07344](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.07344) (search snippet)
- **Isotropy vs clusters (Mickus, Grönroos & Attieh, ACL 2024).** "Point clouds cannot both contain well-defined clusters and be isotropic": maximising IsoScore spreads all pairwise distances, while silhouette/linear-classifier objectives shrink intra-cluster distances; optimising embeddings directly gives Pearson correlations between silhouette and IsoScore of −0.808 (SBERT polarity), −0.878 (SNLI), −0.947 (word2vec POS), −0.978 (supersenses). The paper notes prior work suggesting *within-cluster* isotropy may be a better target but does not test it. — [ar5iv 2402.03191](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.03191); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-short.7)

### Inferences
- Rajaee & Pilehvar is the closest existing instance of "per-cluster origin + per-cluster frame." Its frame is used *subtractively* (delete top-d local PCs) and its clusters are unsupervised k-means over contextual tokens, not hand-picked semantic fields. Their Table-4-style observation (tense dominates within verb clusters) is a small existing precedent for reading linguistic meaning off *local* eigenvectors, but they treat that content as noise for similarity tasks.
- The centering debate (uniform vs frequency-weighted mean; standardisation vs mean-only) shows that even the *global* "where is the origin" question is unsettled; any local-origin proposal inherits the same choice (uniform cluster mean vs frequency-weighted cluster mean).
- Mickus et al. implies that a cluster-wise whitening that erases cluster structure trades classification-type signal for similarity-type signal; a local frame that *keeps* the cluster offset as a coordinate (rather than discarding it) would avoid this trade-off, which no paper here does.

### Gaps
- Cai et al. 2021 full text (LID values, cluster counts, which manifold tools) could not be retrieved — OpenReview and PDF fetches were blocked. Only the abstract and secondary descriptions are cited.
- Mu & Viswanath 2018 "All-but-the-top" was not fetched directly; its numbers appear only as the "global" column in Rajaee & Pilehvar's tables.
- I found no follow-up that extends the cluster-based method itself (searches returned the authors' fine-tuning study and critiques of isotropy metrics, not new cluster-local frames).

## KQ3. Relative / anchor-based representations

### Takeaway
Relative representations (Moschella et al., ICLR 2023) replace absolute coordinates by cosine similarities to a fixed set of anchor samples; the "centre" is an anchor *set* (typically random), the aim is invariance to rotations/rescalings so that different models' latent spaces can be compared or stitched, and the method reads no meaning off directions — it makes neighbourhoods comparable (word2vec vs FastText k-NN Jaccard 0.00 → 0.34–0.39).

### Cited Findings
- **Technique.** r_x = (sim(e_x,e_a1), …, sim(e_x,e_a|A|)) with cosine similarity; anchors are a subset of training data (300 random anchors for the word-embedding experiment; 768 for text stitching, 500 for vision). Uniform random, farthest-point, k-means and frequency-based anchor selection gave "comparable" results. — [ar5iv 2209.15430](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15430)
- **What it buys.** Cosine makes the representation invariant to angle-preserving transformations (rotations, reflections, rescaling) but *not* translations, which they handle with normalisation (e.g., InstanceNorm). — [ar5iv 2209.15430](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15430)
- **Word-embedding result.** ~20K shared words, FastText vs word2vec: absolute spaces Jaccard of k-NN sets 0.00, MRR 0.00, cosine 0.01; relative spaces Jaccard 0.34 (FT→W2V) / 0.39 (W2V→FT), MRR 0.94 / 0.98, cosine 0.86. — [ar5iv 2209.15430](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15430)
- **Stitching results.** Zero-shot encoder/decoder stitching across seeds/architectures/languages: relative stitching far above absolute (e.g., Amazon Reviews coarse ≈72 weighted F1 vs ≈50 absolute); AE/VAE reconstruction MSE up to two orders of magnitude lower. — [ar5iv 2209.15430](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15430)
- **Stated limitations.** Only cosine studied; anchor-set composition vs expressivity "needs more research"; end-to-end training less stable; more anchors did not always help; stitching applied at a single layer only. — [ar5iv 2209.15430](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15430)

### Inferences
- Relative representations are a *global* re-coordinatisation (every point uses the same anchors), not a per-cluster frame. They answer "are two spaces the same up to isometry?", not "what do local directions mean?".
- A per-cluster version (anchors drawn from within each semantic field) is conceivable but I found no paper doing it.

### Gaps
- Norelli et al. 2023 (ASIF) was not fetched; its relation (relative representations across modalities with no training) is not documented here.
- The "Procrustes vs local frames" contrast was not researched beyond Moschella's own framing; no source found that fits a *separate* Procrustes map per cluster for word embeddings.

## KQ4. Mixture and subspace models of word embeddings; evidence that a semantic field occupies a low-dimensional subspace

### Takeaway
Arora et al. show a word vector lies near the *span* of its sense vectors (mean cosine to that span 0.72–0.76) and that ~2000 sparse "discourse atoms" act as directions of semantic fields, each discourse being a combination of 3–4 atoms; Senel et al. show a hand-picked category's words concentrate on a small, category-specific set of dimensions (some categories need few dimensions, others many). Both are evidence for *low-dimensional, category-specific structure*, but neither moves the origin to a field's centre or runs PCA inside a field.

### Cited Findings
- **Arora, Li, Liang, Ma & Risteski 2018 — linearity of senses.** v_word ≈ Σ α_i v_sense_i with α_i ≈ relative sense frequencies (Theorem 2); "multiple word senses reside in linear superposition within the word embedding." Average cosine between a word vector and the span of its sense vectors on SemEval-2010 words: 0.72 (GloVe), 0.73 (skip-gram), 0.76 (SN); a random vector would be far from such a span. — [ar5iv 1601.03764](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1601.03764)
- **Discourse atoms = directions of semantic fields.** Sparse coding (alternating minimisation / k-SVD) with ~2000 unit-vector atoms, ≤5 nonzero coefficients per word, 300-d SN embeddings; ~100 atoms judged meaningless and dropped; atoms labelled by nearest words (e.g., a clothing atom: trousers, blouse, waistcoat; a sports atom: league, championship). A discourse is "a small region rather than a point," typically a combination of 3–4 atoms. — [ar5iv 1601.03764](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1601.03764)
- **Measured effect.** SemEval-2010 WSI: V-measure 6.1 / F-score 58.55 (k=2) and 11.5 / 46.38 (k=5); "police lineup" sense test: 65% precision, 75% recall, "similar to non-native speakers." — [ar5iv 1601.03764](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1601.03764)
- **Athiwaratkun & Wilson 2017.** Each word is a Gaussian mixture whose components capture different meanings, trained with an energy-based max-margin objective; outperforms word2vec skip-gram and single-Gaussian embeddings on word similarity and entailment. — [arXiv 1704.08424](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.08424)
- **Senel et al. 2018 (SEMCAT).** 6,500+ words in 110 categories; for each GloVe dimension i and category j, Bhattacharyya distance between in-category and out-of-category value distributions gives a 300×110 category-weight matrix. Category information is spread across many dimensions (raw GloVe interpretability ≈ random baseline: 12.0% vs 5.1%); some categories need only a few dimensions (math), others many (tools, animals); "metals" is strongest because its words are tightly clustered, "pirate" weakest. Rotating into the category-weight basis raises interpretability to 71.3% (51.5% using category *centroids* instead). — [ar5iv 1711.00331](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.00331)
- **Category-level subspaces in LLM unembeddings (Park et al. 2024).** Extends the linear representation hypothesis to features with no natural contrast (e.g., is_animal) so that categorical concepts become polytopes; proves a relation between concept hierarchy and representation geometry; validated on >900 WordNet concepts in Gemma and LLaMA-3. (Another researcher covers this line in depth; note the overlap only.) — [arXiv 2406.01506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01506)

### Inferences
- Arora's atoms are the nearest thing to "local centres of semantic fields," but they are *directions through the global origin* (unit atoms with sparse nonnegative combination), not origins. The 0.72–0.76 span-cosine result is the strongest quantitative evidence that a word's semantic neighbourhood is approximately a low-dimensional linear subspace (of dimension ≈ number of senses).
- Senel's Bhattacharyya weights are a per-category *frame* (which global axes matter for this category), still expressed in the global origin; their centroid variant I* (51.5%) is the only place a category centroid enters, and it underperforms the dimension-weight method.

### Gaps
- Vilnis & McCallum 2015, Faruqui et al. 2015 (sparse overcomplete) and Subramanian et al. 2018 (SPINE) were not fetched in this session; they are cited in the assignment but I have no verified detail to add beyond the family resemblance to Arora's sparse coding.
- Arora et al. do *not* explicitly claim a topic/semantic field occupies a low-dimensional subspace; the 3–4-atoms-per-discourse statement is the closest.

## KQ5. Local intrinsic dimension and local geometry of embedding neighbourhoods

### Takeaway
Per-point local-ID estimators (Levina–Bickel on k nearest neighbours, TwoNN on L neighbours, LLE reconstruction weights) are routinely applied to word and token embeddings and give low global IDs (GloVe ≈ 25, FastText ≈ 13, word2vec ≈ 25 vs 130 for random 300-d Gaussian) with heavy heterogeneity across tokens; lower-ID tokens form semantically coherent clusters. Nobody in the sources found runs PCA on a word's k nearest neighbours and interprets the components as meanings.

### Cited Findings
- **Kataiwa et al. 2025 (static + LLM token embeddings).** LID_k(x) = [1/(k−1) Σ ln(d_k(x)/d_i(x))]⁻¹ (Levina–Bickel MLE on k nearest neighbours, k = 5), aggregated by MacKay–Ghahramani harmonic mean. IDs: GloVe 24.77, FastText 13.19, word2vec 24.75, random 130.3 (all 300-d); Pythia token embeddings 25–37 for 14M–2.8B (redundancy 72–99%), rising to 78 (6.9B) and 122 (12B). Conjecture (not measured): frequent tokens group "in a semantically meaningful subspace," infrequent ones at the periphery. — [arXiv 2503.02142 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02142)
- **Tulchinskii et al. 2023.** Intrinsic dimension of the manifold underlying a text's set of (RoBERTa) token embeddings averages ≈9 for fluent human text in alphabet-based languages (≈7 Chinese), with AI-generated text ≈1.5 lower; no local-vs-global statement in the abstract. — [arXiv 2306.04723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04723)
- **Valeriani et al. 2023.** ID across transformer layers expands early, contracts in intermediate layers, then plateaus or forms a second shallow peak; semantic information "better expressed at the end of the first peak"; the ID minimum identifies good layers unsupervised. — [arXiv 2302.00294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00294)
- **"Less is More" 2025 (contextual LID).** Measures local dimensions of a contextual LM's latent space; mean local dimension "predicts when the model's training capabilities are exhausted," signals overfitting and grokking; reductions precede performance gains. — [arXiv 2506.01034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01034)
- **Robinson et al. 2025 — token embeddings violate the manifold hypothesis.** Null hypothesis that each token's neighbourhood is "relatively flat and smooth" (fiber-bundle generalisation of a manifold) is "frequently rejected" across open-source LLM token embeddings; prompts containing flagged tokens give less stable responses. — [arXiv 2504.01002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01002); a 2026 preprint summarises their per-token dimension spread as heavy-tailed with median 389 at small radius for GPT-2 — [arXiv 2608.29702](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29702) (search snippet)
- **"Shared Global and Local Geometry of LM Embeddings" 2025.** Local geometry characterised by Locally Linear Embedding (each token as a weighted combination of its k nearest neighbours) plus a simple intrinsic-dimension measure; tokens with lower ID "often form semantically coherent clusters"; token embeddings across models share relative orientations, enabling EMB2EMB linear transfer of steering vectors. — [arXiv 2503.21073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21073)
- **Global-PCA interpretation precedents (not local).** Studies correlate *global* principal components with properties and label components by top positive/negative words (components defined only up to sign), and argue ICA after PCA-whitening gives more interpretable axes. — [Examining Structure of Word Embeddings with PCA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.00114); [Rotations and Interpretability (Russian)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.04662); [ICA universal geometry](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13175) (search snippets)
- **Local PCA = tangent-space estimation in manifold learning.** Local tangent space alignment and related methods compute a tangent basis at each point by PCA on its k nearest neighbours; this machinery exists but the search found no application that reads *semantic* directions off such local PCs for word vectors. — [Tangent space estimation](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1208.1065); [LTSA notes](https://uwaterloo.ca/data-analytics/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/local-tangent-space-embedding.pdf)

### Inferences
- The local-ID literature uses the k-NN neighbourhood only to produce a *scalar* (dimension); it never reports the neighbourhood's principal directions, let alone names them. The learner's proposal (local PCA + interpretation) is the natural next step after these estimators and appears untaken.
- Kataiwa's k = 5 and Robinson's radius-dependent estimates show that "local" dimension depends strongly on neighbourhood scale; any local-frame method must fix the scale (k or radius) and report sensitivity to it.
- The 2026 "hub of short rows" result ties KQ1 and KQ5 together: hubs distort local ID estimates, so a local frame built from a hub's neighbourhood will be unrepresentative unless hubness is corrected first.

### Gaps
- Ansuini et al. 2019 (vision ID across layers) was not fetched.
- No source found that performs PCA on the k nearest neighbours of a word and interprets the components; this is a negative result from four searches, not a proof of absence.

## KQ6. A coordinate system per semantic category (semantic projection, gender subspace, Densifier, category subspaces)

### Takeaway
Three different "category-specific frame" traditions exist: (a) hand-picked *axes* applied to a category (Grand et al.: project animals onto small↔big; median r = 0.47 with human ratings, 32/52 pairs significant); (b) PCA on a hand-picked *set of pairs* to extract one interpretable direction (Bolukbasi: gender subspace; top eigenvalue "significantly larger than the rest"); (c) a learned global orthogonal rotation that packs one property into an ultradense subspace (Densifier). None centres the category at its own mean and interprets the category's own eigenvectors; the axes are either hand-chosen or global.

### Cited Findings
- **Grand, Blank, Pereira & Fedorenko 2022 (semantic projection).** Each feature is a line built from antonym poles with three words each (size: {large, big, huge} vs {small, little, tiny}), the scale being the average of the 9 pairwise difference vectors; category members are projected onto it. One line per feature, reused across categories (members "could be projected onto the same size scale"). 9 categories (animals, clothing, professions, weather, sports, mythological creatures, world cities, US states, first names) × 17 features (age, arousal, cost, danger, gender, intelligence, location, partisanship, religiosity, size, speed, temperature, valence, volume, wealth, weight, wetness) → 52 tested pairs. — [PMC10349641](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10349641/)
- **Measured effect.** GloVe: median Pearson r = 0.47 (95% CI 0.38–0.55; IQR 0.21–0.65), median pairwise order consistency 65%; reliability-adjusted medians r = 0.52 / 74%; both significant in 32 of 52 experiments; best first names × gender r = 0.94, worst cities × cost r = −0.15 and professions × location r = −0.12. Projection onto a single pole, or raw cosine/Euclidean distance to the poles, performed worse than the antonym-difference projection. FastText, word2vec, ELMo, BERT had lower medians. — [PMC10349641](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10349641/)
- **Stated limitations.** Only one-dimensional features; performance "varied considerably" across pairs; antonyms, items and pairings were author-chosen; text-only; concrete English nouns only. — [PMC10349641](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10349641/)
- **Bolukbasi et al. 2016 (gender subspace via PCA on hand-picked pairs).** Ten definitional pairs (she–he, mother–father, woman–man, …); general algorithm: for each defining set D_i compute mean μ_i, deviations w − μ_i, sum outer products into C, bias subspace B = top-k singular vectors of C (for pairs this centres each pair at its own mean); top component g "captures the gender subspace"; first eigenvalue "significantly larger than the rest" and the drop is much sharper than for random unit vectors in 300-d. Used to *neutralize* (remove B-component of neutral words) and *equalize* pairs. — [ar5iv 1607.06520](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1607.06520)
- **Rothe & Schütze 2016 (Densifier).** Learns an orthogonal transformation that "focuses the information relevant for a task in an ultradense subspace" ~100× smaller than the embedding; properties: sentiment, concreteness, frequency; state of the art on lexicon creation and no loss on SemEval-2015 10B sentiment with training an order of magnitude more efficient. Global transformation (one rotation for the whole vocabulary). — [arXiv 1602.07572](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07572)
- **Senel et al. 2018** (see KQ4): per-category dimension weights and a category-centroid variant give a category-specific *frame* in the global origin; 71.3% vs 12.0% interpretability. — [ar5iv 1711.00331](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.00331)
- **Park et al. 2024** (overlap note only): categorical concepts as polytopes; hierarchy ↔ geometry theorem; Gemma/LLaMA-3; >900 WordNet concepts. — [arXiv 2406.01506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01506)

### Inferences
- Grand et al. is the strongest evidence that *restricting to a category* and then reading a direction is informative — but their direction is hand-built and global, and their own worst cases (cost for cities, location for professions) suggest a single global axis does not fit every category, which is exactly the motivation for a per-category frame.
- Bolukbasi's per-pair centering followed by PCA is the closest existing instance of "centre a hand-picked set at its own mean(s), then interpret the top eigenvector." It differs from the learner's proposal in two ways: the centring is per *pair* (not one origin for the field) and only the first component is interpreted (the rest are treated as noise).
- Densifier shows a property can be concentrated in a tiny subspace by a *global* rotation; whether a different rotation per semantic field would do better is untested in the sources found.

### Gaps
- Osgood-style "semantic differential revived with embeddings" (SemAxis, Kozlowski/Taddy/Evans "geometry of culture") were not fetched; they are global hand-picked-axis methods of the Grand type, not per-category frames, but this is unverified here.
- No source found that fits a *different* ultradense subspace / rotation per semantic category.

## KQ7 (synthesis). Has "move the origin to each cluster's centre and interpret the local coordinate system" been done?

### Takeaway
Yes in pieces, no as a whole. The origin-shift exists (localized centering, DisSimLocal, cluster-based isotropy); category-specific frames exist (Senel weights, Grand axes, Bolukbasi subspace, Densifier); local PCA exists (Rajaee & Pilehvar, manifold tangent estimation). I found no work that (i) takes a hand-picked semantic field, (ii) centres it at its own mean, (iii) runs PCA on it, and (iv) interprets the eigenvectors as meanings of that field.

### Cited Findings
- Local origins are used to *fix distances*: localized centering subtracts ⟨x, c_κ(x)⟩ and explicitly "cannot be interpreted as shifting the origin" — [Hara 2015](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/AAAI/2015/localized_centering_reducing_hubness_in_largesample_data__1e4baab2.txt); DisSimLocal subtracts squared distances to local centroids — [Feldbauer & Flexer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7327987/).
- Local frames are used to *remove* directions: Rajaee & Pilehvar delete the top 12–30 PCs per k-means cluster after inspecting that they encode syntax/tense — [ar5iv 2106.01183](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.01183).
- Hand-picked-set PCA is used to extract *one* direction: Bolukbasi's gender direction from pair-centred differences — [ar5iv 1607.06520](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1607.06520).
- Category restriction is used with *hand-built* axes, not eigenvectors: Grand et al., median r = 0.47 — [PMC10349641](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10349641/).
- Neighbourhood PCA exists as tangent-space estimation with no semantic reading — [tangent space estimation](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1208.1065); local-ID papers compute only scalars from the neighbourhood — [Kataiwa 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02142).
- Low-dimensional-field evidence: a word lies at cosine 0.72–0.76 to the span of its sense vectors — [Arora 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1601.03764); category words concentrate on few dimensions for some categories — [Senel 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1711.00331).

### Inferences
- What the existing work "buys": lower hubness skewness (2–8× on text), higher isotropy and +15–37 STS points (cluster-based), model-to-model comparability (relative representations), human-rating correlations ≈0.5 (category projection), interpretability 12% → 71% (category frames).
- Where it falls short for the learner's idea: every local-centre method discards or ignores the local directions; every direction-interpreting method uses hand-built or global axes; isotropy-driven methods are warned (IsoScore, Mickus) that centring is mean-agnostic for isotropy and that erasing cluster structure costs classification signal.
- Explicitly NOT found: interpreting *all* (or the top few) local eigenvectors of a hand-picked semantic field (e.g., 50 animal words centred at their mean) as meanings; a per-field relative representation with field-internal anchors; a per-field Densifier rotation.

### Gaps
- Absence claims rest on roughly a dozen searches plus the papers above; a Google Scholar forward-citation sweep of Rajaee & Pilehvar 2021 and Hara 2015 was not possible with the tools available (search engines returned the authors' own follow-ups and isotropy-metric critiques only).
- Full texts of Cai et al. 2021 and Hara et al. 2016 were inaccessible; any within-cluster PCA interpretation in Cai et al. would change the KQ7 verdict and should be checked by someone with OpenReview access.
